The house

The lake sits in the crater of an old volcano, ringed by three younger ones, and the western shore drops straight into the water. Choq’ is built into that drop: three rooms of glass on a timber frame, stacked down the cliff, with the lake filling every window.

It is a house for mornings. The water is glass until midday, when the wind arrives, and the deck is the place to watch it happen. Evenings are for the stove and the sound of the boats going home.

There is a path down to the water and a boat at the bottom of it. Everywhere on the lake is twenty minutes away.

Inside

  • The deckTimber, over the water, for the whole of the morning
  • The kitchenOpen to the lake, stocked before you arrive
  • The bedroomsThree, each with the view and its own bath
  • The stoveWood-burning, for the evenings, which are cool
  • The waterA private path and a private dock
  • The connectionFibre, in every room, which is rare here

Around the house

  • The lake while it is still glassA boat at first light and a Tz’utujil kitchen in Santiago after.
  • Village to village on footThe path along the shore, with a boat back when the legs give out.
  • A weaver, at her loomAn afternoon in San Juan with the women who still dye with plants.

Practical

  • ArrivalThree hours from the airport by road, then ten minutes by boat. We arrange both.
  • The house teamA cook and a housekeeper, every day.
  • HoursIn from three in the afternoon, out by eleven.
  • ChildrenFrom twelve. The terraces are open to the drop.

Not here

  • No pool. The lake is at the bottom of the steps.
  • No air conditioning. The nights are cool.
  • No car access to the door. The last stretch is by boat, always.
