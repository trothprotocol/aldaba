Guatemala

Lake Atitlán

Mornings are still. Afternoons are not. Everything worth doing here is arranged around that one fact.

The place

Three volcanoes, and water that changes by the hour.

There is no road that matters. The villages sit around the shore and you move between them by boat, which changes how a day feels before it has begun. Santiago is not San Marcos and neither is Santa Catarina; the languages change, the weaving changes, the hour people eat changes.

Around one in the afternoon the xocomil comes down off the ridges and the lake turns. Locals plan their crossings before it and their afternoons after it. So do we.

The days

What a few days here look like.

Written for the house you are staying in, never off a list.

  • 01

    The crossing at dawn

    Glass water, the volcanoes still grey, and a boat that leaves when you are ready rather than when it fills.

  • 02

    Santiago

    A town that has kept its own language and its own saint, seen with somebody who is from there.

  • 03

    The water before noon

    Kayaks, a swim off the rocks, and back on the terrace by the time the wind arrives.

  • 04

    Dusk

    A temazcal heated on the shore, and dinner cooked by a family who have fed people on this lake for a long time.

Practical

Worth knowing before you choose dates.

  • Getting here

    About three hours from Guatemala City, or an hour and a half from Antigua.

  • Best months

    November to March, when the mornings are clearest.

  • How the day runs

    Arrive at the lake before noon. The afternoon wind decides the rest.
