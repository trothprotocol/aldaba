This journey keeps you in one house in Antigua for five nights and takes you out to them one at a time: Fuego you watch, Acatenango you climb in the dark, Pacaya you walk across. Every evening ends back at the house, and every road is ours to drive.

Day by day

  1. Day 1 Into Antigua

    Our car meets you at the airport, and an hour later you are inside the walls of Antigua. The afternoon is yours: the arch, the plaza, a long dinner. The house is home for the week, with Agua standing over the town.

    Drive
    About an hour
    Night
    Our home

  2. Day 2 Coffee before the city wakes

    A finca on the slope above town, picked at first light and cupped under the trees. In the afternoon we check your boots. On a clear evening, go up to the roof and watch Fuego glow across the valley, then bed early: tomorrow starts at midnight.

    Morning
    The finca
    Night
    Our home

  3. Day 3 Acatenango by night

    Your driver collects you at midnight for the trailhead at La Soledad. You climb by headlamp with your guide, through farmland and pine. Near the top Fuego comes into view, erupting below you in the dark, and then the sun comes up over Agua and Pacaya from the summit, 3,976 meters. By early afternoon you are back at the house, in your own bed.

    Walk
    About five hours up, three down
    Night
    Our home

  4. Day 4 A slow day in town

    Sleep as long as you like. Then a walk through the churches and convents that the earthquakes left standing, with a historian rather than a guide, and dinner at a table that does not take bookings.

    On foot
    In town
    Night
    Our home

  5. Day 5 Pacaya on foot

    South to San Francisco de Sales, then up through the forest to the black lava fields below Pacaya’s cone, where the ground is still warm in places. An easier walk than Acatenango, and back at the house for dinner.

    Drive
    About 90 minutes each way
    Walk
    About three hours
    Night
    Our home

  6. Day 6 The road home

    An hour to the airport. Look back as you leave and the mountain you climbed is still there, with Fuego smoking beside it.

    Drive
    About an hour

Good to know

The climb

It starts after midnight and climbs about 1,500 meters in the dark. If you walk in the mountains at home, you will be fine. If not, tell us and we will plan a gentler version.

The cold

The summit is near freezing before dawn, even in the dry season. We send a packing list when you book.

The volcano

Fuego is a working volcano. Most nights it puts on a show; some nights the cloud hides it. We cannot promise the eruption, only the best seat for it.

The weather

If it turns, we hold the climb a day or change the plan. Nobody is sent up the mountain in a storm.
