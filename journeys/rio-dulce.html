Río Dulce

Downriver, under the canopy

The river narrows into a gorge, the walls go up three hundred feet, and the herons move along them in the early light.

Ask about this day

The day

You leave the dock at seven, when the mist is still on the water. Ten minutes downstream the banks close in and the river enters the gorge: limestone walls with the forest growing straight out of them, and the sky a strip overhead.

The boat goes slowly here. There are hot springs coming out of the rock on the north bank, a stretch where the manatees are sometimes seen, and villages reached only by canoe, where the boatman stops if you want him to.

The gorge opens without warning into the Caribbean at Lívingston, a Garifuna town with no road to it. Lunch is tapado, the coconut and seafood stew the town is known for, at a table we have eaten at for years. The boat takes the river back in the afternoon, with the light going the other way.
