Lake Atitlán

The lake while it is still glass

The wind arrives at midday, every day, so the morning belongs to the water.

Ask about this day

The day

The boat is at the dock at six. The lake is flat and the three volcanoes are doubled in it, and for the first hour the only sound is the engine and the birds along the shore. The boatman has been on this water since he was a boy and knows where the swimming is.

The rocks at Santa Cruz drop straight into deep water. You swim, you dry off in the sun on the deck, and the boat turns south toward Santiago, the largest of the lake towns, where the Tz’utujil have lived since long before the Spanish came.

Lunch is in a family kitchen in the town. Fish from the lake, tomatoes from the garden, tortillas made while you watch. There is a walk to the market after if you want it. The boat has you home before the wind gets up, and the afternoon is for nothing at all.
