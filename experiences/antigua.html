Antigua

Coffee before the city wakes

The finca is twenty minutes above town. You arrive in the dark, and the picking starts as the light does.

Ask about this day

The day

The car leaves the house at five. The streets are empty and the volcanoes are still black against a sky that has not decided what colour to be. Twenty minutes up the slope the road turns to dirt, and the finca’s gate is open because they are expecting you.

The pickers start with the light. You walk the rows with the family who have farmed this ground for four generations, and if you want to pick, you pick. By seven the first baskets are at the wet mill and you can follow the cherry through it: the pulping, the tank, the patio where last week’s harvest is drying in the sun.

By eight there is a table set under the trees. Eggs, black beans, tortillas from the comal, and coffee that was on the branch an hour before, roasted the day before, cupped at the table so you can taste what the slope does to it. You are back at the house by eleven, and the day is yours.
